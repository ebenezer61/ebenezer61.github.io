How to read this page

The poster's background is one line of work, but it helps to read it as two tracks on the same axis. The first track is about how to think: what a transformer computes, in terms you can write down. The second is about how to look: what tool lets you see that computation in a real model. The first track makes claims; the second turns them into pictures you can check. The poster uses one result from each cell.

Two definitions recur on the poster and are boxed below where they first appear. A sparse autoencoder (SAE) decomposes the activation vector at one point in the network into sparse, overcomplete, readable directions; "readable" means that scanning a corpus with that direction turns up top examples that share a property a person can describe. A transcoder reads an MLP's input and reconstructs its output through the same kind of dictionary; every feature is a rule "read ej, write dj", and once all 26 layers are replaced and attention is frozen, the influence of one feature on another becomes an inner product, which is what an edge in an attribution graph is.

The timeline

How to think
How to look
2020

Zoom In: An Introduction to Circuits

Olah, Cammarata, Schubert, Goh, Petrov, Carter · Distill · opens the Circuits thread

Three speculative claims: features are the unit, features connect into circuits, analogous circuits recur across models. The microscope metaphor, and the word "circuit" as this field uses it. More on the word.

2021

A Mathematical Framework for Transformer Circuits

Elhage, Nanda, Olsson et al. · Anthropic

An attention head is two linear maps. The QK circuit decides where to look; the OV circuit decides what to move. Heads read and write a shared residual stream. This is the narrow sense of "circuit" and the poster's where / what split. The kernel view of the same thing.

2022

In-context Learning and Induction Heads

Olsson, Elhage, Nanda et al. · Anthropic

A head whose QK circuit finds "the position after the previous occurrence of this token" and whose OV circuit copies what is there: [A][B] … [A] → [B]. Argued to be the main mechanism of in-context learning. A variable name repeated inside a function is exactly this pattern.

Toy Models of Superposition

Elhage, Hume, Olsson et al. · Anthropic

A layer can represent more concepts than it has dimensions by packing them into non-orthogonal directions. So single neurons are the wrong unit to read, and you need a learned dictionary to recover the directions.

more concepts than dimensions → you need a dictionary to see them → the sparse autoencoder
2023

Towards Monosemanticity

Bricken, Templeton, Batson et al. · Anthropic

Train a sparse autoencoder on one layer's activations of a one-layer model and the learned directions are, mostly, things a person can name. The first tool that shows "what is here" at scale.

SAE x ≈ Wdec f(x), with f(x) sparse and wider than x. Decomposes the vector at one point into readable directions: "what is here".
2024

Scaling Monosemanticity

Templeton, Conerly, Marcus et al. · Anthropic

The same tool on a production model (Claude 3 Sonnet). Among the features: one that fires on code errors across several programming languages. The high point of what an SAE alone can show, and also its ceiling: it says what is present, not what the layer did with it.

Transcoders Find Interpretable LLM Feature Circuits

Dunefsky, Chlenski, Nanda · NeurIPS 2024

Instead of encoding a point, learn a sparse dictionary that maps an MLP's input to its output. Each feature is now a computation the layer performs, so features can be chained across layers into a circuit.

Transcoder MLP(x) ≈ Wdec f(x): the encoder reads the MLP input, the decoder rebuilds the MLP output. Feature j is the rule "read ej, write dj": "what this layer computed". With all layers replaced and attention frozen, the effect of feature i on feature j is the inner product ai (di · J · ej), an edge of the attribution graph.

Gemma Scope and JumpReLU SAEs

Lieberum, Rajamanoharan, Conmy et al. · Google DeepMind

Open-weight sparse autoencoders and transcoders for every layer of the Gemma 2 models, with a better activation function (JumpReLU). This is why the poster can run on gemma-2-2b: the dictionaries exist and are public.

2025

Circuit Tracing and On the Biology of a Large Language Model

Ameisen, Lindsey, Pearce et al.; Lindsey, Gurnee, Ameisen et al. · Anthropic · circuit-tracer (open source) · Neuronpedia

Replace every MLP with its transcoder, freeze attention and normalisation, and the whole model becomes linear in the features for one prompt. The graph of feature-to-feature and feature-to-logit effects is the attribution graph: the broad sense of "circuit", prompt-specific, pruned to what matters for the target logits. The tool the poster's main figure comes from.

What the poster takes from each cell

Where (2021, 2022): the attention pattern from the return position, per head, over the tokens of the prompt. QK side only.

What (2023 to 2025): the attribution graph of P4, with every direct effect named against its logit and the aggregate totals beside the pinned subgraph.

Does it matter: scale one feature's activation down and watch the two candidate logits cross. Attribution is an estimate; steering is the check.

Two things worth keeping in mind while reading the graph. A circuit, in either sense, is an approximation found by pruning and thresholds, not a fact about the weights. And an attribution graph is specific to one prompt: change the prompt and you get a different graph, which is why the poster shows four.
