About

I am a PhD student in the Data Science Degree Program at National Taiwan University, where I go by Anna. My research centers on large language models — in particular, on understanding when they should be trusted and how reliably they can express what they do not know.

My earlier work approached machine learning from the systems side, on making deep networks cheaper to run through tensor decomposition, and on building speech recognition systems for languages with little training data. Those projects left me with a lasting interest in the gap between a model's reported confidence and its actual competence — the question I now work on directly.

Research Interests

Large Language Models

Evaluating and improving how large language models reason, generalize, and behave under distribution shift, with an emphasis on what their outputs actually reveal about their internal state.

Uncertainty Estimation

Calibration, confidence, and selective prediction — measuring when a model knows that it does not know, and turning that signal into systems that can abstain or defer rather than fail silently.

Projects

  • Which Variable Does the Model Return?

    work in progress

    An attribution-graph study of how gemma-2-2b completes a Python function. Cut off after return, the model reliably predicts the right local variable — and control prompts show how: each variable name has its own pathway to its own logit, binding sets how much drive that name accumulates, and scaling one pathway down flips the answer. Includes the full interactive graph and a companion page on where the rules break.
